# Practical 6: Privacy Policy Analysis of W3Schools

## Aim
To analyze the privacy policy of W3Schools and identify gaps or areas for improvement.

## Policy Selected
- **Organization:** W3Schools
- **Website:** [W3Schools](https://www.w3schools.com)
- **Privacy Policy:** [W3Schools Privacy Policy](https://www.w3schools.com/about/about_privacy.asp)
- **Version analyzed:** Last updated 16 September 2026

## Requirements
- Internet connection
- Web browser
- W3Schools Privacy Policy and Cookie Policy

## Procedure
1. Open and read the W3Schools Privacy Policy.
2. Divide the policy into its main sections.
3. Record what the policy says in each section.
4. Judge whether each section is clear, complete and user-friendly.
5. Identify gaps and weaknesses.
6. Suggest improvements.

## Overview of the Policy
- The policy divides users into **Customers** (paid, ad-free) and **Free Users** (ad-supported).
- It states that it exists "to tell you what we collect and what we do with it, rather than to be agreed to", and that consent for analytics and personalized ads is asked separately through a consent tool.
- It covers the website, services and mobile apps, and has separate sections for the EEA/UK and the United States, plus a sub-processor table.

## Section-by-Section Analysis

| Policy Section | What the Policy Says | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Information collected | Contact, account, transaction, support and voluntary data, plus telemetry, usage, device and service-generated data, cookies and data from sub-processors. Data from different sources may be combined. | Clear. Categories are listed, but "combine information" is not explained in detail. |
| How information is used | Operating and improving services, support, account administration, marketing, security and legal compliance. | Clear. Purposes are listed separately. |
| Targeted advertising (Free Users) | Partners create persistent identifiers that recognize the same device across websites. Signed-in users who consent may get an identifier derived from their hashed email address. | Detailed and honest, but the practice is privacy-intrusive. |
| Sharing of information | Sub-processors (cloud, billing, support, security, analytics), legal reasons, and business transfers. Ad partners are split into processors (Actirise, Viously, LiveRamp) and independent controllers (e.g., ID5, Criteo, Google). | Strong transparency. Independent controllers make it hard for users to follow where data goes. |
| User rights | Opt out of marketing, update account data, delete the account, manage cookies and vendor consent. Extra rights depend on location. | Clear. Some rights depend on the user's location. |
| Security | Administrative, technical and physical safeguards. The policy admits no measure is foolproof and says W3Schools is not responsible for sub-processors' handling beyond what it provides or receives. | Generic. No details such as encryption standards or certifications are given. |
| Retention | Kept for the "minimum period necessary", then deleted or anonymized. | Weak. No specific periods are stated. |
| Data deletion | "Delete My Account" in the profile page, with deletion within 30 days. It is permanent, cannot be transferred, and there are no refunds. | Clear and easy to follow. |
| Third-party services | Links to other sites are not controlled by W3Schools. Users should read their policies. | Standard disclaimer. |
| Mobile apps | Same data types collected. Purchases go through Apple or Google, and RevenueCat manages subscriptions. | Clear and recently added. |
| International transfers | Data may be processed in the USA or Europe. Safeguards include Standard Contractual Clauses and the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. | Good legal safeguards. |
| Children | Generally not for under-16s. No knowing collection from under-13s except as COPPA allows. | Partial. No age verification process is described. |
| Policy changes | Policy is reviewed periodically, and a "Last Update" date is shown at the bottom. Users are encouraged to review it. | Weak. There is no promise to notify users directly of important changes. |
| Jurisdiction sections | EEA/UK legal bases and rights, and US (CCPA-style) rights, with response times of 30 days (EEA) and 45 days (US). | Good for those regions. No section for India. |
| Contact | One email address (help@w3schools.com). | Weak. No Data Protection Officer or named privacy contact. |

## Strengths of the Policy
- Separate consent for analytics and personalized advertising, which can be withdrawn at any time.
- Per-vendor control of advertising partners through the consent tool.
- Clear explanation of the email-derived advertising identifier, including that it is still personal data.
- Paid plans and classroom accounts are excluded from advertising.
- Complete sub-processor table with company, location and purpose.
- Simple, step-by-step account deletion with a 30-day timeline.
- Regional sections for the EEA/UK and the United States.
- Recent update that includes the mobile apps.

## Gaps and Weaknesses
- **Vague retention:** "Minimum period necessary" does not tell users how long each type of data is kept.
- **Possible inconsistency:** The introduction says the policy is meant to inform rather than to be agreed to, but the transfer section says users consent to processing in the USA or Europe by using the services.
- **Independent controllers:** Some ad partners decide on their own how data is used, so users must contact each of them separately.
- **No India coverage:** There is no section on the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and no grievance contact for Indian users.
- **Children:** The under-16 guidance does not match laws such as the DPDP Act, which treats under-18s as children, and no age verification is described.
- **No breach notification:** The policy does not describe what happens or how users are told if their data is breached.
- **Change notification:** Users are only encouraged to check the date, not notified directly.
- **Limited security detail:** The safeguards are described only in general terms.
- **Single contact:** Only one general email is provided, with no named privacy officer.

## Recommendations
- State specific retention periods for each category of data.
- Remove the inconsistency between "not to be agreed to" and "you consent" wording.
- Add a section for Indian users covering the DPDP Act, 2023, with a grievance officer.
- Align age limits with local laws and add age verification and parental consent steps.
- Add a data breach notification section explaining how and when users will be told.
- Notify registered users by email when the policy changes in a significant way.
- Give more detail on security measures (for example, encryption in transit and at rest).
- Name a Data Protection Officer or privacy contact.
- Give users one place to exercise rights against all advertising partners.

## Result
The privacy policy of W3Schools was successfully analyzed section by section. Its strengths, gaps and areas for improvement were identified.

## Conclusion
The W3Schools Privacy Policy is detailed and transparent, especially on consent, advertising, sub-processors and account deletion. However, it has gaps in retention periods, children's data, breach notification, change notification, security detail and coverage for Indian users. Addressing these would make the policy clearer and more protective of users.
